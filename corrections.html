CORRECTIONS AND EDITORIAL STANDARDS

// Last updated: November 8, 2026

How the Collective's briefings and the AI vs Physician paper are made, how we fix mistakes, and who pays for the site.

// How the evidence pages are made

  • Briefings (the blog) are drafted by an AI agent three times a week from studies and news it finds and opens that day. An item is kept only if its link appeared in what the agent actually read.
  • The AI vs Physician paper is redrafted every month by an AI agent from a register of studies. The studies it is built on are checked against the paper's full text or abstract, and every sentence must cite a study in the register.
  • Nothing is published until a clinician in the Collective has read the draft, opened its sources and approved it. Our standard is that a second clinician reads each draft too; we say so on a draft when that has not been possible.
  • Drafts never contain patient information, and the AI agent is never given any.
  • These pages summarise research for education. They are not clinical guidelines. Always read the original study before relying on it.

// Corrections

  • Anyone can report a mistake with the "Report an error" link under each briefing and at the end of the paper, or by emailing the address below. Please say what is wrong and, if you can, the source that shows it.
  • We aim to reply within 7 days, and to fix a clear factual error as soon as it is confirmed.
  • A correction that changes a result, a number or a verdict is noted where it was made, with the date, rather than changed silently. The paper also lists its changes under "What changed".
  • If a study we covered is retracted or corrected by its journal, we mark it as such and update or remove what we wrote about it.
  • Spelling and formatting fixes are made without a note.

// Independence, funding and conflicts of interest

  • The Collective is an independent, volunteer-run group. It has no sponsors, takes no advertising, and is not paid by anyone to cover, rank or recommend a study, tool or company.
  • Its running costs (web hosting, email and the AI used to draft the evidence pages) are paid by its volunteer organizers.
  • Anyone who reviews a draft must say if they have a financial tie (employment, consulting, shares, funding or payment) to a company whose product it covers, or are an author of a study it covers, and leave that item to another reviewer.
  • Tools on the Tools page are made by members. Listing a tool is not an endorsement, and a member may have a stake in a tool they share.
