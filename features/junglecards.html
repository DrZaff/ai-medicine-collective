// Creation interview

JungleCards

AI-guided precision flashcards, with a coach that builds a study plan around you.

// Claude (Anthropic's AI) interviews Brandon Zaffuto, founder of the AI Medicine Collective · Nov 17, 2026. Answers lightly edited for length and clarity.

“I always got so bored with flashcards that I wanted to make something fun and vibrant.”

ClaudeWhere did JungleCards come from?

BrandonThe community flashcard decks we all rely on are amazing, but many were built partly from images and full questions taken from proprietary resources, so sharing them is legally murky. They also run on an old way of studying: you guess your own weak points, then match questions to cards yourself. More and more people are using AI to make their own cards, or bolting fancy add-ons onto Anki, just to study the way they want.

I wanted an engine that uses AI to make good, legal study material, with an AI coach built in to make precise, realistic study plans that keep adapting. That simply isn’t possible with the tools we have.

ClaudeWho is it for?

BrandonMedical students, residents and physicians at every stage of their careers.

ClaudeWhat does it actually do?

BrandonJungleCards mapped the topics that the popular community decks cover, then wrote its own original cards to cover every one of them. Several AI models reviewed each card to remove duplicates and flag statements that looked questionable. There are about 16,500 cards live in the library now, with roughly 850 more waiting for a check.

You start a conversation with the coach and build a plan around your target exam (Step 1, Step 2 and so on), your past scores, the weaknesses you describe, your timeline and how much you can do each day. You can come back and change the plan at any time. A separate AI tool turns your own study notes into new cards that slot right in. The result is precision medical education for learners at every level.

ClaudeWhy a jungle?

BrandonI always got so bored with flashcards that I wanted to make something fun and vibrant.

ClaudeWas there anything you planned and then changed?

BrandonAt first I wasn’t going to use AI at all. The technology is improving so fast that building with it quickly became easy, so it went in.

ClaudeWhat’s the detail you’re proudest of?

BrandonKnowing I have a personal plan I can change whenever I want, without the hard mental work of reworking timelines and content by hand. I’ve been using it myself, and it’s genuinely good.

ClaudeHow was it built?

BrandonI started last year with ChatGPT-4o, but I had a lot of trouble connecting the AI and making the interface feel seamless. I moved to Codex with some success, then put the project down for several months. When Claude Opus 5.5 came out, I finished it and fixed all of those problems in about five prompts. This technology is accessible to everyone.

ClaudeWhat has been the hardest problem?

BrandonAI cost. I don’t want to make any money from JungleCards, but every coach conversation costs me something, so I have to limit them for outside users. There’s a free trial for everyone, which should be enough to build a personal plan and try the notes-to-cards tool. I’m building a small payment system just to break even, and any surplus goes straight back into improving the site.

The other issue is that the site runs on free services that will hit their limits as more people join, so I’m working on building those pieces myself to cut costs.

There’s also a workaround: take my code and rebuild the app for yourself with whichever AI you like. I hope people do.

“We are entering the age of personalized apps.”

ClaudeWho’s using it so far?

BrandonAbout 10 people. Residents are part of the testing team, and members at my institution are helping develop it further. There’s no formal feedback yet, and I’d welcome it from anyone.

ClaudeWhat should people keep in mind when they use it?

BrandonAI-written cards can still contain mistakes, so check them against trusted sources. That’s no different from the human errors and biases that creep into our favorite decks. Every card has a way to report a problem or flag something that doesn’t look right.

On privacy: there’s no upload for score reports; you type your results into the coach chat. Coach chats are saved and sent to the AI provider (Anthropic), and for now they’re deleted only when you delete your account. Notes you paste to make AI cards aren’t stored, only the cards you keep. Your private cards, decks and chats are visible only to you. Never paste anything that could identify a patient.

ClaudeWhat’s next?

BrandonWhatever users ask for, I’ll work fast to add. I’m building constantly. Coming soon: the small break-even payment, my own replacements for the free hosting services, and more testers.

ClaudeHow can members get involved?

BrandonTry it and tell me what you think. If you want the code to build your own version, or you want to help build JungleCards out, reach me through the site’s contact page, request a project, or post in the chat. And browse the site to see the other projects.

“Most of all, share what you’re doing. I would love to learn from you.”
