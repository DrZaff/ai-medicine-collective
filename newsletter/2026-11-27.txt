// THE ONE THING
How Medical Students Use AI for Learning: Cross-Sectional, Survey-Based Quality Improvement Evaluation
AI use is now almost universal among early learners, so the practical gap is verification, not access. Educators can make checking AI output an explicit, assessed skill in the curriculum and in faculty development.
https://mededu.jmir.org/2026/1/e109763

// AI VS PHYSICIAN
This week: AI outperformed physicians
Human vs Artificial Intelligence in Detecting Coronary Occlusions in ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction: Retrospective Study
Subtle occlusion MIs that don't meet STEMI criteria are a known blind spot, and this suggests AI ECG tools could serve as a safety net. But the AI's 60% specificity means false alarms, and a study of 33 ECGs cannot settle the question, so learners should treat it as a reason to sharpen their own OMI pattern recognition, not to hand over the decision.
https://cardio.jmir.org/2026/1/e88399
