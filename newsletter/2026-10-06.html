> A student-built roadmap for what medical students should learn about AI, and when

// 2026-10-03

What and when: a Delphi study identifying AI competencies and learning objectives for medical students — Frontiers in Digital Health

A team at the University of Navarra School of Medicine in Spain ran a four-round modified Delphi process between May 2024 and January 2025. Fourteen medical students and two professors proposed and rated possible learning objectives, and 10 international experts in medical education and AI then checked the results. The panel settled on 7 competencies and 37 learning objectives that medical graduates need for working with AI, plus suggested timing for teaching them across undergraduate medical education.

> AI picks nightly teaching cases for radiology residents, and a student-built study app gains users

// 2026-10-05

AI Tool Finds and Fixes Training Gaps for Radiology Residents — NYU Langone News (press release on a study in Academic Radiology)

NYU Langone faculty listed the conditions residents should see in their first three years across five imaging subspecialties, with a target number of encounters for each. They then had ChatGPT-4o read the summary sections of each resident's daily reports and pick three to five teaching cases every night, favoring conditions the resident had rarely seen; residents reviewed these at their workstations and discussed them with supervisors. The press release says the AI found exposure gaps and suggested the right case types with more than 90 percent accuracy, and that residents saw a wider range of pathology without losing time on real clinical cases.

UCLA alumnus turns medical school frustrations into AI study app 'Neural Consult' — Daily Bruin

This student-newspaper profile covers Neural Consult, an AI study tool for medical and other patient-facing students, co-created by a medical student at the Keck School of Medicine of USC. The article puts its user count at about 100,000 (over in one place, nearly in another) and says it grew out of the founder's struggle to combine patient-interaction skills with practical knowledge while studying. It is a feature story, not an evaluation, and it reports no data on learning outcomes or accuracy.
