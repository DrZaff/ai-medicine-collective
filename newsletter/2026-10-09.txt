Award-Winning AI Curriculum Keeps Residents on Their Toes
Programs building their own AI teaching get a concrete model for one of the hardest design problems: teaching residents to use AI in a way that supports their clinical reasoning instead of causing deskilling. The model also allows for very uneven starting knowledge across a residency class.
https://medicine.stanford.edu/news/stories/2026/10/award-winning-ai-curriculum-keeps-residents-on-their-toes.html

// AI VS HUMAN
This week: Mixed results
Divergent impacts of explainable AI for dermatological diagnosis on clinicians versus lay people
In this skin diagnosis task, AI help raised PCPs' accuracy, but medical students and lay people were more easily misled when the AI was wrong. Polished LLM explanations did not make the advice more useful to doctors. Educators can use this to show trainees why they should commit to their own diagnosis before checking the AI's answer.
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04553-w
Running tally: AI ahead 0, humans ahead 0, even or mixed 1.
